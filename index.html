Ringed Transit Simulator

Shape the ring, send the planet across a limb-darkened star, and compare two transits. Epoch 2 can differ by ring precession, ring evolution, a timing shift, the motion of an exomoon or the rotation of a spotted star; the lower plot shows whether that change rises above TESS 2-minute noise. False positives can be added too: a dust clump with a tail that shears and fades by epoch 2, and light from other stars in the aperture.

Physics follows ringed_transit.py: quadratic limb darkening, circular orbit, opaque planet, thin ring of normal optical depth τ seen through exp(−τ/|cos θ|), planet hiding the ring behind it. The planet disk is computed exactly; the ring is rasterised (about 0.0005% precision after the plot settles). An optional moon orbits the planet–moon barycentre (period from Kepler's law), adds its own limb-darkened dip and shifts the planet's transit time; moon–planet overlap is counted once, ring–moon overlap is ignored. Star spots are dark caps on the rotating, limb-darkened star: they dim it as they rotate into view, and a planet, ring or moon crossing one blocks less light (spot-crossing bump); with "Normalise each transit" on, each epoch is divided by a line fitted to its out-of-transit flux, as a pipeline would; off, flux is relative to the unspotted star. SNR assumes white noise at the chosen 2-minute scatter: ring SNR = √Σ(ringed − bare)² / σ for one transit; epoch-change SNR adds the noise of both transits. The dust clump moves along the same orbit, delayed by the chosen time; its optical depth is an asymmetric Gaussian along the orbit (separate leading and trailing lengths) times a Gaussian across it, the star is integrated on a grid, and dust hides no star spots. By epoch 2, Keplerian shear from the radial spread Δa/a stretches the clump by 3π(Δa/a)·a per orbit, conserving its cross-section, minus the per-orbit loss. Dust mass = 4/3 ρ s ∫τ dA for grains of radius s and ρ = 2.5 g cm⁻³. Light from other stars adds a constant: observed = (1 − c)·model + c.
